This case study looks at a series of 3D product animations our team produced for a US hyperbaric chamber company. The client is not named, and we only share what is already in our public portfolio, so there are no sales figures here. What we can show is the brief, the approach and the lessons for any company selling equipment that is hard to film.

## The challenge: equipment that is hard to film

Hyperbaric chambers are large, heavy medical devices. Filming them well means a big space, controlled studio lighting, a finished unit of every model, and a crew. Even then, a camera struggles with the things buyers want to see: the inside of the chamber, how the door and seating work, and how one model compares with the next.

A company with a range of models has a second problem: every film needs to look like it belongs to the same brand, even when the products are different sizes and shapes.

## What we produced

Our team built the series as one production rather than five separate jobs:

- **One film per model.** Each of the five animations presents a single chamber, so a product page, an ad or a sales call can show exactly the unit being discussed.
- **One shared studio look.** The same lighting, materials and camera style run through all five films, so the range reads as one family.
- **Photoreal models.** Each chamber was modeled, lit and animated in Blender, with camera moves designed around the shape of that product.
- **Reusable assets.** Because the scenes are built in 3D, a new model or a design change starts from the existing setup instead of a new shoot.

## What medical equipment makers can take from this

1. **Plan the series, not the single film.** If you sell several models, agree one look, one length and one shot structure before modeling starts. Each extra film then costs less and matches the others.
2. **Use 3D for what a camera cannot show.** Interiors, cutaways, doors opening and seating positions are where 3D earns its cost.
3. **Start from engineering files.** CAD files make models accurate and faster to build; drawings and photos work when CAD is not available.
4. **Keep claims out of the animation.** For medical devices, let the film show the product and leave health claims to your approved marketing and regulatory copy.
5. **Ask for the scene files.** Owning the native 3D files means future edits, new colours and new models do not start from zero.
