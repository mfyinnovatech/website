Almost every ERP conversation we have in Kuwait and the Gulf ends up at the same shortlist: **Odoo, SAP Business One and Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central**. All three are serious products used by thousands of small and mid-sized companies. The right choice depends on your size, your existing systems, the countries you operate in and, more than anything, the partner who implements it.

A note on bias first: MFY Innovatech is an official Odoo partner. We have tried to keep this comparison fair, and we say plainly where SAP Business One or Business Central is the better fit.

## Odoo vs SAP Business One vs Business Central at a glance

| | Odoo | SAP Business One | Dynamics 365 Business Central |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best fit | Growing SMEs that want sales, inventory, manufacturing, accounting, HR and e-commerce in one system | SMEs inside an SAP group, or with an established SAP partner | Finance-led SMEs already standardised on Microsoft 365 |
| Licence model | Per user per month, priced by country; open-source core | Perpetual licence plus maintenance, or cloud subscription through partners | Per user per month (Essentials, Premium, Team Members) |
| Typical licence level | Usually the lowest of the three | Usually the highest of the three | In between |
| Scope in one product | Very wide: CRM, sales, inventory, MRP, accounting, HR, payroll, website, e-commerce, helpdesk | Core finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, production; more areas through add-ons | Core finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, projects; manufacturing in Premium; more through AppSource |
| Arabic interface and documents | Yes, including right-to-left | Arabic documents typically through partner layouts | Arabic documents typically through partner localization |
| GCC localization | Saudi Arabia, UAE and Oman documented; Kuwait and Qatar listed | Mainly through partner add-ons | Kuwait and Saudi Arabia partner-localized, hosted in the UAE |
| Customisation | Python modules; very flexible | SDK and add-ons | AL extensions; strong Microsoft tooling |

## What the licences cost

Licences are only part of an ERP budget, but they are the part that repeats every year. These are list or partner-reported figures; always confirm with a quote for your country and user mix.

| Odoo | Per user per month, set by country. Check the price for your country on Odoo's site. Our [5-year cost study](/blog/saas-vs-custom-software-cost-ai-era) used the US Custom plan at $61 per user per month | [Odoo pricing](https://www.odoo.com/pricing) |

| SAP Business One | No public price list. Partners report about $110 to $190 per user per month for cloud, or about $1,700 to $3,200 per user perpetual plus around 18 to 20 percent a year maintenance | [CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/erp/sap-business-one/) (partner-reported, indicative) |

For **50 full users**, that puts yearly licences at roughly **$37,000 for Odoo** (at the US Custom price), **$48,000 for Business Central Essentials** ($66,000 for Premium) and roughly **$66,000 to $113,000 for SAP Business One cloud**. Over five years, implementation, integrations and support usually add as much again or more. Our [ERP budget guide](/blog/how-to-plan-an-erp-budget) shows how to build the full number.

## What a GCC company must check, whichever ERP it picks

The product brochure is the same everywhere. These are the requirements that decide whether an ERP actually works in Kuwait and the Gulf.

### Arabic and bilingual documents

Invoices, quotations, delivery notes and statements often need Arabic and English side by side. In Saudi Arabia, tax invoices must be in Arabic (they can be bilingual). Ask to see your own invoice printed in both languages, with Arabic shaping and right-to-left layout correct, before you sign.

### Three-decimal KWD and multi-currency

The Kuwaiti dinar has three decimals (1,000 fils). Prices, rounding, tax-free totals and reports must all keep the third decimal. Groups with companies across the GCC also need SAR, AED, QAR, BHD, OMR and USD, with exchange-rate handling and consolidation.

### Saudi ZATCA Phase 2

Saudi e-invoicing Phase 2 requires integration with ZATCA's Fatoora platform: structured XML, cryptographic stamps, QR codes and clearance or reporting. ZATCA is rolling it out in waves; [Wave 25](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/saudi-arabia-announces-25th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration) covers taxpayers whose taxable revenue exceeded SAR 187,500 in any year from 2022 to 2025, with integration due by 1 February 2027. Odoo's [Saudi localization](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/master/applications/finance/fiscal_localizations/saudi_arabia.html) includes ZATCA e-invoicing; Business Central and SAP Business One usually rely on partner add-ons.

### UAE e-invoicing

The UAE's mandate starts with businesses earning AED 50 million or more on [1 January 2027](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-uae-phased-implementation-of-e-invoicing-beginning-january-1-2027.html), and other businesses on 1 July 2027, through an accredited service provider. Ask every vendor how its UAE invoices will reach that provider, and who maintains the connection when the rules change.

### Kuwait specifics

Kuwait has not introduced VAT so far, which keeps invoicing simpler, but Kuwaiti companies with Saudi or UAE operations inherit those rules. Day to day, what matters most in Kuwait is [KNET and local bank integration](/blog/erp-integrations-kuwait-knet-banks-ecommerce), Arabic documents and payroll with end-of-service indemnity, leave and PIFSS contributions.

### Where the data lives

Business Central online environments for Kuwait and Saudi Arabia are [hosted in the UAE Azure geography](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/compliance/apptest-countries-and-translations). Odoo and SAP Business One can be hosted by the vendor, a partner or on your own servers. If you are a regulated business, such as financial services, healthcare or government supply, confirm the hosting location against your sector's rules.

## When Odoo is the better fit

- You want **one system** for sales, inventory, purchasing, manufacturing, accounting, HR, payroll, website and e-commerce, instead of an ERP plus several add-ons.
- **Cost per user matters** because headcount will grow, or many staff need occasional access.
- Your processes are **specific** and you want custom modules that you own, written in a mainstream language (Python).
- You trade or manufacture across **several GCC countries** and want multi-company and multi-currency in one database.

## When SAP Business One is the better fit

- Your **parent company runs SAP** S/4HANA and wants subsidiaries on SAP for group reporting and familiarity.
- You have a **strong local SAP Business One partner** with years of history in your industry and country.
- Your auditors, lenders or group controllers **expect SAP** and the higher licence cost is acceptable.

## When Business Central is the better fit

- Your company is **standardised on Microsoft 365**, Teams, Excel, Power BI and Entra ID, and wants the ERP inside that world.
- The ERP is **finance-led**: accounting, purchasing, inventory and projects matter more than e-commerce or HR.
- Your partner has a **current Kuwait or Saudi localization** on AppSource and you are comfortable with UAE hosting.

## US companies with GCC operations

Many of the companies we work with are headquartered in the USA with a subsidiary or distributor in the Gulf, or the reverse. In that case the deciding questions change:

1. **Can one database hold both companies?** All three support multi-company, but check intercompany transactions and consolidation in a demo.
2. **Can each country stay compliant?** US sales tax in one company, ZATCA or UAE e-invoicing in another, and Arabic documents where needed.
3. **Who supports both time zones?** A partner who can answer during the US working day and the Gulf working day saves weeks over a project.

## How to choose in five steps

1. **Write down your ten most important processes**, including your messiest order, return or month-end task.
2. **Shortlist two products, not three.** Use the "best fit" lists above to drop one early.
3. **Ask each partner to run your worst order** in a demo, with your data, in Arabic and in KWD. Not their demo data.
4. **Compare five-year cost, not year-one licences.** Include implementation, integrations, support, upgrades and internal time. Our [ERP timeline guide](/blog/how-long-does-an-erp-implementation-take) helps you estimate the project length too.
5. **Choose the partner as carefully as the product.** Most ERP failures are delivery failures. Our checklist for [choosing an Odoo implementation company in Kuwait](/blog/best-odoo-implementation-company-in-kuwait) applies to any ERP partner.
