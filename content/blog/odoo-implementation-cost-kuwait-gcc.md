When a company in Kuwait or the wider Gulf asks "how much does Odoo cost?", it usually means one number. There are really two: the Odoo licence, which is published and easy to check, and the implementation, which depends on your company and is where most budgets go wrong. This guide explains both, the costs people forget, and how to get a reliable number before you sign.

## How much does Odoo cost? The short answer

**Odoo costs a licence fee per user per month, set by Odoo for each country, plus a one-off implementation cost that is usually the larger part of year-one spending.** Ongoing costs after go-live are the licence, hosting if you do not use Odoo Online, and support.

| Cost | What it covers | Who sets it | When you pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Odoo licence | All apps, plus support, hosting and maintenance on Odoo's plans | Odoo, per country, billed in USD in the Gulf | Monthly or yearly |
| Implementation | Discovery, configuration, data migration, integrations, customisation, training, go-live | Your implementation partner | Per phase, before and at go-live |
| Hosting (Custom plan only) | Odoo.sh or your own cloud or servers | Odoo (Odoo.sh) or your cloud provider | Monthly |
| Support after go-live | Fixes, small changes, new users, month-end help | Your partner | Monthly or per hour |
| Compliance extras | E-invoicing connections where required (Saudi Arabia, UAE) | Odoo localization, partner, provider | One-off and ongoing |

## Odoo licence pricing in Kuwait and the GCC

**Odoo publishes three options, and in the Gulf it bills in US dollars at a price set for each country.** These are the plans on Odoo's [pricing page](https://www.odoo.com/pricing), checked on 18 November 2026:

| Plan | What you get | Hosting |
|---|---|---|
| One App Free | One app, unlimited users | Odoo Online |
| Standard | All apps, per user per month | Odoo Online only |
| Custom | All apps plus Odoo Studio, multi-company, external API | Odoo Online, Odoo.sh or on-premise |

A few details change the final number:

- **Yearly billing is cheaper than monthly**, and Odoo offers a first-year discount on the users ordered at the start.
- **Light users** cost less than regular users, which suits staff who only fill in timesheets or approvals.
- **Odoo.sh hosting is charged separately** when you choose the Custom plan and host on Odoo.sh.
- **Support, hosting and maintenance are included** in all plans, according to Odoo.

### How to get your exact Odoo price in two minutes

Odoo's [pricing configurator](https://www.odoo.com/pricing-configurator) calculates the price for the country you are in. Enter the number of regular users, choose yearly or monthly billing and the plan, and it shows the per-user price, the first-year discount and a total you can print as a quote. Do this from Kuwait, the UAE or Saudi Arabia on the day you build your budget, because Odoo adjusts prices by region and over time.

We do not quote licence prices from third-party websites in this guide, because the ones we checked for the Gulf disagree with each other.

## What drives the Odoo implementation cost

**The implementation cost depends on seven things: apps, users, data, integrations, customisation, training and support.** The software is the same for everyone; the work around it is what you are paying for.

| Driver | Why it adds cost | How to keep it down |
|---|---|---|
| Number of apps | Each app needs workshops, configuration and testing | Start with the apps that run your money: sales, inventory, purchasing, accounting |
| Number of users and departments | More roles, access rights, approvals and training | Phase by department; train key users who train the rest |
| Data migration | Cleaning customers, products, opening stock and balances takes time | Clean data before the project, not during it |
| Integrations | KNET and payment gateways, banks, e-commerce, marketplaces, delivery apps | Use standard connectors first; build custom ones only where volume justifies it |
| Customisation | Custom modules must be built, tested and maintained through every upgrade | Keep phase one close to standard Odoo |
| Training and change | People need to learn new ways of working | Budget for it explicitly; it is the first thing cut and the first thing missed |
| Support after go-live | The first month-end close and the first weeks of real use | Agree who is on call and what it costs before go-live |

How long the project takes drives cost too. A focused first phase typically goes live in 8 to 14 weeks; our guide to [ERP implementation timelines](/blog/how-long-does-an-erp-implementation-take) explains what makes projects run longer.

## Costs specific to Kuwait and the GCC

**Gulf companies have a few costs that generic Odoo price guides leave out.** Ask every partner how they handle each one.

- **Kuwaiti dinar with three decimals.** The dinar has 1,000 fils, so currency precision and rounding must be set before the first transaction. Fixing it after go-live means correcting live accounting data.
- **Arabic and bilingual documents.** Quotations, invoices and delivery notes often need Arabic and English on the same page. Ready templates save days.
- **KNET and local banks.** Accepting KNET and matching payments and bank statements automatically is integration work; see our [KNET payment gateway guide](/blog/knet-payment-gateway-integration-kuwait).
- **Payroll rules.** Kuwait's end-of-service indemnity, leave and social security for Kuwaiti employees need localized salary rules and a parallel payroll run before you switch.
- **E-invoicing where it applies.** Saudi Arabia requires ZATCA Phase 2 integration, which Odoo's [Saudi localization](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/master/applications/finance/fiscal_localizations/saudi_arabia.html) supports. The UAE makes e-invoicing mandatory from 1 January 2027 for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more and from 1 July 2027 for others, through an Accredited Service Provider ([UAE Ministry of Finance](https://mof.gov.ae/einvoicing/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-uae-phased-implementation-of-e-invoicing-beginning-january-1-2027.html)). Kuwait has no VAT in 2026, so the priority there is a tax setup that can absorb VAT later without a re-implementation.
- **Multi-company and multi-country.** Groups with entities in Kuwait, the UAE and Saudi Arabia need the Custom plan for multi-company, plus a separate localization for each country. Odoo lists Kuwait, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Oman among its [fiscal localizations](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/finance/fiscal_localizations.html).

This is general information, not tax advice. Confirm which rules apply to your entity with your accountant.

## The hidden costs most budgets miss

1. **Dirty data.** Duplicate customers, products without units of measure and opening stock nobody trusts add weeks.
2. **Customisation debt.** Every custom module has to be tested again at each Odoo version upgrade.
3. **Licence growth.** Adding users, a second company or moving to the Custom plan for Studio or the API changes the yearly licence.
4. **The first month-end close.** It is where implementations succeed or fail; make sure support is included.
5. **Internal time.** Your team's hours in workshops, testing and training are real cost, even if they are not on an invoice.

## Self-implementation, Odoo's Success Pack or a partner

**Odoo itself recommends its Success Pack for companies under 50 users and a local partner above that,** according to the implementation options in its pricing configurator. In practice:

- **Self-service** works for one or two apps with simple processes and a team member who has time to learn Odoo properly.
- **A Success Pack** gives you a block of hours from Odoo's own consultants, which suits standard processes.
- **A local partner** makes sense when you need KWD accounting set up correctly, Arabic documents, payroll, integrations with KNET or banks, custom modules, or support in Gulf working hours.

## How to get a reliable Odoo quote

1. Write down your processes, users per department and the systems Odoo must connect to.
2. Get your licence price from Odoo's configurator for your country.
3. Ask partners for a discovery call, then a written scope and estimate per phase.
4. Compare scopes, not totals: apps, data migrated, integrations, customisation, training and post-go-live support.
5. Be wary of anyone who gives a fixed price on the first call. Our guide to [choosing an Odoo implementation company in Kuwait](/blog/best-odoo-implementation-company-in-kuwait) lists the questions to ask.
