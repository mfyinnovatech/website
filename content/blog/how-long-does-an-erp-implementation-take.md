"How long will it take?" is the second question every company asks about a new ERP, right after "how much will it cost?". Vendor brochures say weeks. Industry horror stories say years. The honest answer sits in between, and it depends far more on your scope, your data and your decisions than on the software you pick.

## How long does an ERP implementation take? Typical timelines by company size

| Company profile | Typical scope | Typical timeline to go-live |
|---|---|---|
| Small (up to about 50 users, one company) | Core modules, standard flows, few or no integrations | 2 to 4 months |
| Mid-sized (about 50 to 200 users) | Several departments, some customisation, 2 to 5 integrations | 4 to 9 months |
| Large, multi-company or multi-country | Many modules, consolidation, several languages and tax rules | 9 to 18 months or more |
| First phase of a phased project | The one area in the most pain, on standard flows | 8 to 14 weeks |

These are realistic ranges for projects with a written scope and an engaged client team. Projects without either sit at the top of each range, or above it.

## The phases, and how long each one takes

For a mid-sized company implementing a first phase, the work usually breaks down like this. Several phases overlap; data migration in particular runs alongside everything else.

| Phase | What happens | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery and scope | Map how the business really works, agree what is in phase one, write it down with success measures | 2 to 4 weeks |
| 2. Configuration and design | Set up companies, chart of accounts, warehouses, products, pricing and approval rules on standard flows | 4 to 8 weeks |
| 3. Data migration | Clean and load customers, suppliers, products, open orders and opening balances; trial loads before the final one | Starts in week 1, runs throughout |
| 4. Integrations and customisation | Connect payment gateways, banks, e-commerce, shipping or other systems; build only what standard cannot do | 2 to 6 weeks, in parallel |
| 5. Testing and training | Run real scenarios end to end, then train each role on its own data | 2 to 4 weeks |
| 6. Go-live and support | Final data load, switch over, close support through the first month-end | 2 to 4 weeks |

A published example: a US distributor with 4 warehouses and more than 3,000 SKUs, running on QuickBooks and spreadsheets, went live on inventory, purchasing and manufacturing in 10 weeks. Stock-outs fell to near zero within two months, and month-end close went from 4 days to half a day.

## What makes ERP implementations take longer

**Scope that keeps growing.** Every "while we are at it" request adds design, build and testing time. A scope that is not written down cannot be protected. This is the same root cause behind most [ERP projects that go over budget](/blog/why-erp-projects-go-over-budget).

**Dirty data found late.** Duplicate customers, products with several codes, stock nobody trusts. Discovered in the last month, it delays go-live by weeks.

**Slow decisions.** The implementation team cannot decide your pricing rules, approval chain or costing method. When every decision waits for a meeting, the calendar slips one week at a time.

**Customising before trying the standard.** Each customisation needs to be built, tested and maintained. Copying the old system screen by screen is the slowest route to go-live.

**Integrations nobody listed.** A bank file format, a payment gateway or a marketplace connector that appears mid-project becomes a small project of its own.

**Key people who are not available.** The finance lead who knows the opening balances is also closing the month. Without protected time, testing and training stall.

## Timeline factors specific to Kuwait and the Gulf

- **Bilingual documents.** Arabic and English invoices, quotations and reports need to be designed and tested in both languages.
- **Local integrations.** KNET, local bank payment files and e-commerce platforms each need their own setup and testing. See [connecting your ERP to KNET, banks and e-commerce in Kuwait](/blog/erp-integrations-kuwait-knet-banks-ecommerce).
- **Payroll rules.** End-of-service indemnity, leave entitlements and PIFSS contributions must be configured and run in parallel before switching payroll over.
- **Calendar.** Ramadan, Eid holidays and the summer travel period slow decisions and user testing. Plan milestones around them rather than through them.
- **Multi-company and multi-currency.** Groups with entities across the GCC need intercompany rules and consolidation, which belong in the plan from day one.

## How to shorten your ERP timeline without cutting corners

1. **Go live in phases.** Start with the area in the most pain, stabilise it, then add the next one.
2. **Write the scope down.** Modules, integrations, reports, data and what "done" means, signed before configuration starts.
3. **Start data cleanup in week one,** with a template and a named owner on your side.
4. **Stay close to standard in phase one.** Customise only what the standard flow genuinely cannot do.
5. **Name one decision-maker** with protected time and a weekly decision meeting.
6. **List every integration up front** and test each one with real transactions.
7. **Train by role, before go-live,** on your own data.
8. **Pick the go-live date carefully:** start of a month or quarter, away from year-end and peak season.
